### The Creative Process: A Checklist for World-Building

Think of this as a series of steps. You don't have to complete one before starting the next, but it helps to keep them in mind to structure your thoughts.

*   **[x] Step 1: Establish the Core Mood & Central Conflict.**
    *   What does it *feel* like to live in this world? Is it hopeful, grim, adventurous, mysterious?
    *   What is the main struggle? (e.g., Survival vs. Nature, Order vs. Chaos, Technology vs. Humanity).
*   **[x] Step 2: Define the "Magic" and the "Monsters".**
    *   Flesh out "Techno Wizardry." How does it work? Who can use it? What are its limits?
    *   Flesh out the "Daemons." Where did they come from? What do they want? How do they manifest?
*   **[x] Step 3: Sketch Out the World's History.**
    *   What was the "before time"? What were the "troubles" that led to now? See [[WorldLore]].
*   **[x] Step 4: Define the Factions.**
    *   Who holds power? Create major factions with competing goals. See [[Faction_BrainStorm]].
*   **[x] Step 5: Outline a Starting Location.**
    *   Don't build the whole world at once. Detail a single city, region, or space station.
*   **[x] Step 6: Create Adventure Hooks.**
    *   Write down 5-10 simple one-sentence adventure ideas based on everything you've built.

### Expanding Your "Cyber-Fantasy" Ideas

Let's use that checklist to build on your concepts.

#### **1. Core Mood & Central Conflict**

*   **The Mood:** The overall feel of the world is **Adventurous**. We are explicitly avoiding a grim, dark, or dystopian setting. The challenges are real and dangerous, but the focus is on exploration, discovery, and the thrill of delving into the unknown. It's a world of high stakes and high rewards, where brave souls can make a difference and uncover wonders.
*   **Central Conflict:** The core struggle is the **Survival of Man vs. the Unknown of Machine.** Humanity is not necessarily losing, but they are in a constant struggle against a world filled with technology that has become unpredictable, alien, and often hostile. This isn't a simple war; it's a tense coexistence where the "unknown" of the machine could represent incomprehensible intelligence, chaotic glitches, or the emergent, wild nature of a bygone technological ecosystem.
